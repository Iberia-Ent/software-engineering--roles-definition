## Purpose

Clarify independent ownership of regular Backend scope and the evidence that distinguishes L3
from reliable but still guided execution.

## Audience

Product Owner, Product Manager, Delivery Lead, and Technical Lead. Backend Developer, Coach, and
Mentor use the linked criteria to validate technical judgement.

## Overview

L3 owns regular Backend scope, chooses sound solutions, and works independently within the squad.
Ownership covers understanding the change, explaining trade-offs, validating behaviour, and
communicating residual risks. It does not mean making every decision without collaboration.

## Product-team context

A representative situation is delivering a business change that spans an API, persistence, and
an event-driven interaction. The Developer should explain how boundaries, contracts, retries,
or consistency choices affect the business flow and its failure cases, using technical review
where necessary. This example illustrates the scope rather than prescribing a mandatory project.

Product stakeholders should be able to rely on proactive updates and a reasoned explanation of
options. Community contribution and mentoring begin to matter alongside individual delivery:
an improvement that helps the squad avoid a recurring issue is more informative than simply
finishing another ticket with the same known problem.

## Evidence and progression

Review independent changes, design explanations, contract or E2E evidence, and examples of
quality improvement shared with others. The [L3 gate and L3-to-L4 evidence](./Backend_Java_Career_Path_Chapter.md#l3-promotion-gate)
call for ownership, sound decisions, quality, and active community contribution.

Technical influence should be visible in outcomes: better boundaries, clearer quality evidence,
or peers who can apply an improvement. Suggested evidence is not an extra scoring system; the
source criteria and technical calibration govern progression.
