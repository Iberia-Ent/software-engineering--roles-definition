# QA Career Path

> Iberia Tech · QA (Globant) · 2026-11-10

## Purpose

Provide the detailed competency reference used to discuss QA growth in Academy product teams.
The criteria retain the supplied November 2026 matrix; this introduction explains how
stakeholders should read it without creating extra promotion conditions.

## Audience

The primary audience is the Product Owner, Product Manager, Delivery Lead, and Technical Lead.
QA Manager, Coach, Mentor, and QA Engineer use the same criteria to validate technical depth,
quality judgement, and professional development.

## Overview

Each competency describes a progression from foundational knowledge to strategic influence.
Read across the areas to understand a level: autonomy in one automation tool alone does not
demonstrate autonomous QA ownership. The expected product contribution includes prioritisation,
clear communication, and evidence-based challenge of weak quality conditions.

Use the [profile overview](./README.md) to navigate by seniority. During review, connect the
criteria to actual refinements, test decisions, defects, risk discussions, and shared
improvements. Product stakeholders explain the observed delivery effect; QA leadership and
technical reviewers confirm whether the evidence demonstrates the competency.

The experience ranges below are reference values, not eligibility dates. Promotion is based on
demonstrated impact and the criteria later in this document. The
[May chapter](./quality_assurance_career_path_chapter.md) is a separate, older source with some
different supervision wording and a pending L5 calibration statement; it should not be silently
merged into this matrix.

## Levels

| Level | Name | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Junior | 0-12 months |
| L2 | Jr Advance | 12-24 months |
| L3 | Semi Senior | 24-42 months |
| L4 | SSR Advance | 42-60 months |
| L5 | Senior | 60+ months |

## Proficiency scale

The same scale applies to every competency. Proficiency is required at L3 (Semi Senior).

- **L1 · FOUNDATION · Basic knowledge**: Knows the fundamentals of every competency and what to apply at each moment. Executes with guidance.
- **L2 · FOUNDATION+ · Guided practice**: Applies the fundamentals consistently in day-to-day work, with limited supervision.
- **L3 · PROFICIENT · Autonomous**: Applies every competency autonomously and owns the QA scope of the squad with evidence-based judgment.
- **L4 · ADVANCED · Squad/domain reference**: Goes beyond own delivery: improves practices, mentors other QAs and multiplies across teams.
- **L5 · EXPERT · Strategic reference**: Sets standards and direction across products, domains and suppliers.

#### Foundation to autonomous contribution (L1–L3)

| Area | L1 · Junior | L2 · Jr Advance | L3 · Semi Senior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Role Focus | Learns fundamentals and disciplined execution | Delivers recurring QA work reliably | Owns QA scope as a real squad QA owner |
| Test Design & Prioritization | Follows defined priorities, learns critical flows | Designs standard test cases with basic risk-based prioritization | Defines coverage by risk, change impact and critical flows |
| Automation & Tooling | Initial exposure to the approved stack, with guidance | Builds simple scripts and maintains automated assets | Works independently with the approved stack |
| Quality Ownership / Authority | Raises issues early through the proper channel | Challenges obvious gaps, does not accept weak deliverables | Can stop, reject or escalate with evidence |
| Communication & Collaboration | Communicates clearly and documents properly | Participates actively in refinement and testing discussions | Collaborates proactively and explains risks with evidence |
| Innovation / Practice Contribution | Learns QA standards and ways of working | Applies standards and shares learnings in the team | Active CoP participation and improvement proposals |
| AI-Enabled QA | Uses AI only under supervision, no code generation | Uses AI with controlled scope and reviews outputs | Validates and improves AI outputs and prompts |
| Performance Testing | Knows concepts and test types, flags performance impact | Guided scenarios and basic K6 scripts | Designs scenarios and K6 scripts autonomously |
| Accessibility (A11y) | Knows WCAG basics and which stories need A11y | Runs axe-core scans and basic screen reader checks | Plans and runs manual and automated A11y, gives go/no-go |
| AWS & Cloud Awareness | Knows basic cloud and AWS vocabulary | Follows technical conversations with Devs, TLs and Cloud | Uses product architecture on AWS to define scope and risks |
| Observability (Dynatrace) | Knows observability basics, navigates Dynatrace with guidance | Uses Dynatrace during test execution and follows traces | Uses Dynatrace proactively every cycle, with evidence in go/no-go |

### Test Design & Prioritization

Coverage is valuable when it addresses the most important product risks. This area distinguishes
following an agreed scope from deciding what matters, challenging redundant work, and setting
the principles used by other teams.

<a id="criteria-test-design-prioritization-l1-junior-foundation-basic-knowledge"></a>

#### L1 · Junior — FOUNDATION · Basic knowledge

<a id="qa-item-l1-test-design-prioritization-01"></a>
- Understands the difference between executing tests and designing them
<a id="qa-item-l1-test-design-prioritization-02"></a>
- Can follow pre-defined priorities with guidance
<a id="qa-item-l1-test-design-prioritization-03"></a>
- Starts to understand critical flows and business impact
<a id="qa-item-l1-test-design-prioritization-04"></a>
- Does not automate without direction

<a id="criteria-test-design-prioritization-l2-jr-advance-foundation-guided-practice"></a>

#### L2 · Jr Advance — FOUNDATION+ · Guided practice

<a id="qa-item-l2-test-design-prioritization-01"></a>
- Designs standard test cases for regular stories
<a id="qa-item-l2-test-design-prioritization-02"></a>
- Identifies basic coverage gaps
<a id="qa-item-l2-test-design-prioritization-03"></a>
- Starts applying simple risk-based prioritization
<a id="qa-item-l2-test-design-prioritization-04"></a>
- Understands that not everything should be automated

<a id="criteria-test-design-prioritization-l3-semi-senior-proficient-autonomous"></a>

#### L3 · Semi Senior — PROFICIENT · Autonomous

<a id="qa-item-l3-test-design-prioritization-01"></a>
- Defines coverage based on business risk, change impact, and critical flows
<a id="qa-item-l3-test-design-prioritization-02"></a>
- Distinguishes what should and should not be automated
<a id="qa-item-l3-test-design-prioritization-03"></a>
- Avoids redundant testing
<a id="qa-item-l3-test-design-prioritization-04"></a>
- Thinks before executing or automating

<a id="criteria-test-design-prioritization-l4-ssr-advance-advanced-squaddomain-reference"></a>

#### L4 · SSR Advance — ADVANCED · Squad/domain reference

<a id="qa-item-l4-test-design-prioritization-01"></a>
- Optimizes scope and testing effort
<a id="qa-item-l4-test-design-prioritization-02"></a>
- Challenges low-value automation and weak coverage decisions
<a id="qa-item-l4-test-design-prioritization-03"></a>
- Reduces redundancy and increases ROI of QA activities
<a id="qa-item-l4-test-design-prioritization-04"></a>
- Aligns coverage with delivery goals and product risk

<a id="criteria-test-design-prioritization-l5-senior-expert-strategic-reference"></a>

#### L5 · Senior — EXPERT · Strategic reference

<a id="qa-item-l5-test-design-prioritization-01"></a>
- Defines prioritization principles, quality criteria, and automation direction
<a id="qa-item-l5-test-design-prioritization-02"></a>
- Ensures QA effort is invested where it creates highest value
<a id="qa-item-l5-test-design-prioritization-03"></a>
- Establishes the standard for risk-based decision making
<a id="qa-item-l5-test-design-prioritization-04"></a>
- Prevents wasteful or low-value QA activity


### Automation & Tooling

Automation should provide dependable evidence and reduce repeated effort where it creates value.
The progression concerns understanding, maintainability, and appropriate use of the approved stack,
not introducing tools or scripts for their own sake.

<a id="criteria-automation-tooling-l1-junior-foundation-basic-knowledge"></a>

#### L1 · Junior — FOUNDATION · Basic knowledge

<a id="qa-item-l1-automation-tooling-01"></a>
- Understands Iberia’s approved QA automation ecosystem at a basic level
<a id="qa-item-l1-automation-tooling-02"></a>
- Has initial exposure to Karate, WebdriverIO, and TypeScript
<a id="qa-item-l1-automation-tooling-03"></a>
- Can execute simple supported tasks with guidance
<a id="qa-item-l1-automation-tooling-04"></a>
- Understands that automation must follow purpose, not volume

<a id="criteria-automation-tooling-l2-jr-advance-foundation-guided-practice"></a>

#### L2 · Jr Advance — FOUNDATION+ · Guided practice

<a id="qa-item-l2-automation-tooling-01"></a>
- Contributes to maintenance of automated assets in the approved stack
<a id="qa-item-l2-automation-tooling-02"></a>
- Starts building simple scripts with guidance using Karate, WebdriverIO, and TypeScript
<a id="qa-item-l2-automation-tooling-03"></a>
- Follows defined conventions and reusable patterns
<a id="qa-item-l2-automation-tooling-04"></a>
- Understands the need to adapt to new approved tools over time

<a id="criteria-automation-tooling-l3-semi-senior-proficient-autonomous"></a>

#### L3 · Semi Senior — PROFICIENT · Autonomous

<a id="qa-item-l3-automation-tooling-01"></a>
- Works independently with the approved stack: Karate, WebdriverIO, TypeScript
<a id="qa-item-l3-automation-tooling-02"></a>
- Maintains and creates automation assets with quality and reusability in mind
<a id="qa-item-l3-automation-tooling-03"></a>
- Adapts to new short-/mid-term tools adopted by Iberia
<a id="qa-item-l3-automation-tooling-04"></a>
- Promotes correct use of approved frameworks inside the squad

<a id="criteria-automation-tooling-l4-ssr-advance-advanced-squaddomain-reference"></a>

#### L4 · SSR Advance — ADVANCED · Squad/domain reference

<a id="qa-item-l4-automation-tooling-01"></a>
- Strengthens automation quality, maintainability, and reusability

- Promotes adoption of approved tooling and engineering good practices
<a id="qa-item-l4-automation-tooling-03"></a>

<a id="qa-item-l4-automation-tooling-04"></a>
- Identifies improvement opportunities in frameworks, libraries, or automation patterns

<a id="criteria-automation-tooling-l5-senior-expert-strategic-reference"></a>

#### L5 · Senior — EXPERT · Strategic reference

<a id="qa-item-l5-automation-tooling-01"></a>
- Sets direction for automation consistency, adoption, and evolution across squads and suppliers
<a id="qa-item-l5-automation-tooling-02"></a>
- Evaluates strategic tooling needs with governance in mind
<a id="qa-item-l5-automation-tooling-03"></a>
- Sponsors the correct evolution of the approved QA stack and its future extensions
<a id="qa-item-l5-automation-tooling-04"></a>
- Ensures tool adoption supports maintainability, scalability, and Iberia-wide consistency


### Quality Ownership / Authority

Product teams need QA to make weak conditions and unresolved risks visible, including when
that means challenging a delivery proposal. The progression describes how evidence-based
authority grows from early reporting to an established quality reference.

<a id="criteria-quality-ownership-authority-l1-junior-foundation-basic-knowledge"></a>

#### L1 · Junior — FOUNDATION · Basic knowledge

<a id="qa-item-l1-quality-ownership-authority-01"></a>
- Raises issues and blockers early with support
<a id="qa-item-l1-quality-ownership-authority-02"></a>
- Understands that QA is expected to speak up when quality is unclear
<a id="qa-item-l1-quality-ownership-authority-03"></a>
- Escalates concerns through the proper channel

<a id="criteria-quality-ownership-authority-l2-jr-advance-foundation-guided-practice"></a>

#### L2 · Jr Advance — FOUNDATION+ · Guided practice

<a id="qa-item-l2-quality-ownership-authority-01"></a>
- Raises quality concerns more independently
<a id="qa-item-l2-quality-ownership-authority-02"></a>
- Challenges obvious gaps in requirements or acceptance criteria
<a id="qa-item-l2-quality-ownership-authority-03"></a>
- Does not silently accept weak deliverables

<a id="criteria-quality-ownership-authority-l3-semi-senior-proficient-autonomous"></a>

#### L3 · Semi Senior — PROFICIENT · Autonomous

<a id="qa-item-l3-quality-ownership-authority-01"></a>
- Challenges incomplete or weak quality conditions
<a id="qa-item-l3-quality-ownership-authority-02"></a>
- Can stop, reject, or escalate when evidence does not support confidence
<a id="qa-item-l3-quality-ownership-authority-03"></a>
- Understands QA authority as part of the role, not as an exception

<a id="criteria-quality-ownership-authority-l4-ssr-advance-advanced-squaddomain-reference"></a>

#### L4 · SSR Advance — ADVANCED · Squad/domain reference

<a id="qa-item-l4-quality-ownership-authority-01"></a>
- Exercises clear quality authority with confidence
<a id="qa-item-l4-quality-ownership-authority-02"></a>
- Pushes back on weak decisions using evidence and professionalism
<a id="qa-item-l4-quality-ownership-authority-03"></a>
- Defends standards across stakeholders
<a id="qa-item-l4-quality-ownership-authority-04"></a>
- Makes quality position visible and respected

<a id="criteria-quality-ownership-authority-l5-senior-expert-strategic-reference"></a>

#### L5 · Senior — EXPERT · Strategic reference

<a id="qa-item-l5-quality-ownership-authority-01"></a>
- Is recognized as the quality authority
<a id="qa-item-l5-quality-ownership-authority-02"></a>
- Sets the standard for saying No when quality, readiness, or risk conditions are not acceptable
<a id="qa-item-l5-quality-ownership-authority-03"></a>
- Protects the product, the customer, and delivery integrity through sound quality judgment
<a id="qa-item-l5-quality-ownership-authority-04"></a>
- Drives escalation when needed

<a id="qa-item-l2-communication-collaboration-04"></a>
- Shows reliability in recurring collaboration

### AI-Enabled QA

AI support does not transfer accountability away from the QA Engineer. These criteria describe
increasingly capable but governed use, with human review, appropriate supervision, and attention
to whether the output genuinely improves the QA activity.

<a id="criteria-ai-enabled-qa-l1-junior-foundation-basic-knowledge"></a>

#### L1 · Junior — FOUNDATION · Basic knowledge

<a id="qa-item-l1-ai-enabled-qa-01"></a>
- Uses AI only under supervision and not for code generation
<a id="qa-item-l1-ai-enabled-qa-02"></a>
- Learns how AI can support QA activities in a controlled way
<a id="qa-item-l1-ai-enabled-qa-03"></a>
- Can collaborate in simple prompt adjustments or reviews under guidance from a more senior QA
<a id="qa-item-l1-ai-enabled-qa-04"></a>
- Understands that AI output must always be reviewed and never replaces understanding or judgment

<a id="criteria-ai-enabled-qa-l2-jr-advance-foundation-guided-practice"></a>

#### L2 · Jr Advance — FOUNDATION+ · Guided practice

<a id="qa-item-l2-ai-enabled-qa-01"></a>
- Uses AI with controlled scope and guidance
<a id="qa-item-l2-ai-enabled-qa-02"></a>
- Reviews simple AI-generated outputs and detects obvious gaps or inaccuracies
<a id="qa-item-l2-ai-enabled-qa-03"></a>
- Can support prompt refinement under supervision
<a id="qa-item-l2-ai-enabled-qa-04"></a>
- Does not rely on AI as a substitute for analysis, test design, or technical understanding

<a id="criteria-ai-enabled-qa-l3-semi-senior-proficient-autonomous"></a>

#### L3 · Semi Senior — PROFICIENT · Autonomous

<a id="qa-item-l3-ai-enabled-qa-01"></a>
- Validates and improves AI-generated scenarios, scripts, and analyses
<a id="qa-item-l3-ai-enabled-qa-02"></a>
- Refines prompts to get better outcomes
<a id="qa-item-l3-ai-enabled-qa-03"></a>
- Starts optimizing AI-assisted workflows for usefulness and efficiency
<a id="qa-item-l3-ai-enabled-qa-04"></a>
- Applies professional judgment before adoption

<a id="criteria-ai-enabled-qa-l4-ssr-advance-advanced-squaddomain-reference"></a>

#### L4 · SSR Advance — ADVANCED · Squad/domain reference

<a id="qa-item-l4-ai-enabled-qa-01"></a>
- Integrates AI into QA workflows in a controlled and useful way
<a id="qa-item-l4-ai-enabled-qa-02"></a>
- Optimizes prompts and usage patterns

### Performance Testing

Performance work connects expected user behaviour and service load with acceptance criteria.
Stakeholders should understand the risks being explored and the implications of the results,
not simply whether a load-test script executed successfully.

Every QA, from Junior level, knows the basics of performance and what to test at each moment.
Proficiency is required at L3 (Semi Senior); from L4 the QA becomes a reference and multiplier.

<a id="criteria-performance-testing-l1-junior-foundation-basic-knowledge"></a>

#### L1 · Junior — FOUNDATION · Basic knowledge

<a id="qa-item-l1-performance-testing-01"></a>
- Understands core performance concepts: response time, throughput, concurrency, percentiles and SLOs
<a id="qa-item-l1-performance-testing-02"></a>
- Knows each test type and its purpose: smoke, load, stress, spike, soak and scalability
<a id="qa-item-l1-performance-testing-03"></a>
- Identifies when a story has performance impact and flags it in refinement
<a id="qa-item-l1-performance-testing-04"></a>
- Runs existing K6 scripts with guidance and reads basic results

<a id="criteria-performance-testing-l2-jr-advance-foundation-guided-practice"></a>

#### L2 · Jr Advance — FOUNDATION+ · Guided practice

<a id="qa-item-l2-performance-testing-01"></a>
- Turns simple stories into performance scenarios (users, ramp-up, duration, think time, acceptance criteria) with review
<a id="qa-item-l2-performance-testing-02"></a>
- Writes basic K6 scripts (requests, checks, thresholds, stages) with guidance
<a id="qa-item-l2-performance-testing-03"></a>
- Chooses the right test type for the change being delivered
<a id="qa-item-l2-performance-testing-04"></a>
- Reads p90/p95, error rate and throughput against the agreed SLOs

<a id="criteria-performance-testing-l3-semi-senior-proficient-autonomous"></a>

#### L3 · Semi Senior — PROFICIENT · Autonomous

<a id="qa-item-l3-performance-testing-01"></a>
- Designs performance scenarios autonomously from stories, DoR non-functional requirements and SLOs
<a id="qa-item-l3-performance-testing-02"></a>
- Builds clean, reusable K6 scripts (checks, thresholds, stages, test data) that Devs can evolve
<a id="qa-item-l3-performance-testing-03"></a>
- Selects and runs smoke, load, stress, spike or soak tests based on risk and release
<a id="qa-item-l3-performance-testing-04"></a>
- Analyses results, detects degradations and reports them with evidence
<a id="qa-item-l3-performance-testing-05"></a>
- Stops or escalates when SLOs are not met

<a id="criteria-performance-testing-l4-ssr-advance-advanced-squaddomain-reference"></a>

#### L4 · SSR Advance — ADVANCED · Squad/domain reference

- Integrates K6 in CI/CD with thresholds acting as quality gates
<a id="qa-item-l4-performance-testing-03"></a>
- Correlates results with observability data (APM, logs, infra metrics) to support root-cause analysis
<a id="qa-item-l4-performance-testing-04"></a>
- Reviews scenarios and scripts and mentors other QAs
<a id="qa-item-l4-performance-testing-05"></a>
- Challenges missing or unrealistic SLOs in the DoR

<a id="criteria-performance-testing-l5-senior-expert-strategic-reference"></a>

#### L5 · Senior — EXPERT · Strategic reference

<a id="qa-item-l5-performance-testing-01"></a>
- Sets performance standards, SLO criteria and reusable K6 assets across products
<a id="qa-item-l5-performance-testing-02"></a>
- Defines performance as a Quality Gate between environments

Proficiency is required at L3 (Semi Senior); from L4 the QA becomes a reference and multiplier.

### AWS & Cloud Awareness

Cloud awareness helps QA recognise how product architecture changes the scope and risks of
testing. It supports informed collaboration with Developers, Technical Leads, and Cloud Engineers;
it does not redefine the QA role as infrastructure implementation.

QAs are not expected to be AWS experts. They are expected to understand what Devs, TLs,
Architecture and Cloud teams are talking about, so they can identify risks, scope testing
correctly and route issues to the right people.

<a id="criteria-aws-cloud-awareness-l1-junior-foundation-basic-knowledge"></a>

#### L1 · Junior — FOUNDATION · Basic knowledge

<a id="qa-item-l1-aws-cloud-awareness-01"></a>
- Knows basic cloud and AWS vocabulary: regions, accounts and environments, serverless, containers, managed services
<a id="qa-item-l1-aws-cloud-awareness-02"></a>

<a id="qa-item-l1-aws-cloud-awareness-03"></a>

<a id="criteria-aws-cloud-awareness-l2-jr-advance-foundation-guided-practice"></a>

<a id="qa-item-l2-aws-cloud-awareness-03"></a>
- Asks the right questions when a change touches cloud components

<a id="criteria-aws-cloud-awareness-l3-semi-senior-proficient-autonomous"></a>

### Observability (Dynatrace)

Observability helps the team understand what the product actually does during testing and
operation. The progression moves from guided use of signals to proactive, evidence-based quality
decisions and a shared observability model, rather than relying only on post-incident analysis.

Observability is used proactively, as part of testing and quality gates, to detect degradation
before it reaches production, not only reactively after an incident. Iberia's observability
platform is Dynatrace.

<a id="criteria-observability-dynatrace-l1-junior-foundation-basic-knowledge"></a>

#### L1 · Junior — FOUNDATION · Basic knowledge

<a id="qa-item-l1-observability-dynatrace-01"></a>
- Understands what observability is (metrics, logs, traces, events) and the difference between proactive and reactive use
<a id="qa-item-l1-observability-dynatrace-02"></a>
- Navigates Dynatrace with guidance to find a service, its response times and its errors
<a id="qa-item-l1-observability-dynatrace-03"></a>
- Attaches basic Dynatrace evidence to defects

<a id="criteria-observability-dynatrace-l2-jr-advance-foundation-guided-practice"></a>

#### L2 · Jr Advance — FOUNDATION+ · Guided practice

<a id="qa-item-l2-observability-dynatrace-01"></a>
- Uses Dynatrace during test execution to check errors, response times and failure rates of the services under test
<a id="qa-item-l2-observability-dynatrace-02"></a>
- Follows a distributed trace to locate where a request fails
<a id="qa-item-l2-observability-dynatrace-03"></a>
- Compares behaviour between environments before raising a defect

<a id="criteria-observability-dynatrace-l3-semi-senior-proficient-autonomous"></a>

#### L3 · Semi Senior — PROFICIENT · Autonomous

<a id="qa-item-l3-observability-dynatrace-01"></a>
- Uses Dynatrace proactively in every test cycle: confirms new changes add no errors, latency or resource regressions before moving to the next environment
<a id="qa-item-l3-observability-dynatrace-02"></a>
- Correlates functional and performance test results with Dynatrace data (traces, services, databases, real user monitoring) to give root cause with evidence
<a id="qa-item-l3-observability-dynatrace-03"></a>
- Agrees with Devs the key signals and SLOs to watch for a story or release
<a id="qa-item-l3-observability-dynatrace-04"></a>
- Includes observability evidence in go/no-go decisions

<a id="criteria-observability-dynatrace-l4-ssr-advance-advanced-squaddomain-reference"></a>

#### L4 · SSR Advance — ADVANCED · Squad/domain reference

<a id="qa-item-l4-observability-dynatrace-01"></a>
- Builds Dynatrace dashboards and alerts for the squad/domain to detect quality degradation early
<a id="qa-item-l4-observability-dynatrace-02"></a>
- Integrates observability checks into quality gates and CI/CD (e.g. SLO evaluation after deployments or load tests)
<a id="qa-item-l4-observability-dynatrace-03"></a>
- Uses synthetic monitors for critical flows in INT and PRE
<a id="qa-item-l4-observability-dynatrace-04"></a>
- Mentors other QAs on proactive observability

<a id="qa-item-l5-observability-dynatrace-02"></a>
- Sets standards for SLO-based quality gates between environments
<a id="qa-item-l5-observability-dynatrace-03"></a>

## Promotion gates

### L1 → L2

- Reliable execution
- Clear defect reporting
- Basic risk awareness
- Basic tooling exposure
- Supervised AI usage only
- Basic Performance, A11y, Cloud & Observability knowledge

### L2 → L3

- Consistent test design
- Basic risk-based prioritization
- Automation contribution in approved stack
- Challenges obvious gaps
- Limited supervision
- Performance, A11y, Cloud & Observability proficiency

### L3 → L4

- Strong QA ownership
- Independent use of approved stack
- Can say No / escalate with evidence
- Active CoP participation
- Improvement proposal or PoC contribution
- Performance, A11y, Cloud & Observability reference in the squad

### L4 → L5

- Visible quality leadership
- Cross-team influence
- Mentoring and supplier alignment
- Leads proposals / PoCs / adoption
- Defines or improves AI usage patterns
- Sets Performance, A11y, Cloud & Observability standards

## Promotion criteria

- Hard skills demonstrated consistently
- Soft skills observable in real squad behavior
- Autonomy already shown at the next level
- Quality ownership demonstrated in practice
- Responsible use of automation and AI
- Community contribution required from Semi-Senior onwards
- Promotion based on impact, not only tenure
- Performance, A11y, Cloud & Observability: basics from L1, proficiency at L3

## Non-negotiables

- QA must think before testing and prioritize before automating
- Automation is not a goal by itself
- QA must understand and progressively adopt Iberia’s approved automation stack
- QA must communicate clearly across Dev, TL, PO, business, and QA peers
- QA must not work in isolation
- QA has the authority and responsibility to say No
- QA is expected to challenge weak quality conditions
- QA must help spread good practices beyond individual execution
- AI usage must always be responsible, supervised according to seniority, and never replace judgment, validation, or accountability
- Every QA knows what to test in performance and accessibility, and when, from Junior level onwards

## Anti-patterns

- Automating everything without risk analysis
- Using Karate, WebdriverIO, or TypeScript mechanically without understanding quality purpose
- Executing tests without understanding business impact
- Waiting for others to define all priorities
- Accepting unclear requirements without challenge
- Avoiding difficult conversations or failing to escalate quality risks
- Poor communication with Dev, business, or QA peers
- Working in isolation from other squads or QAs
- Using AI outputs without critical review or optimization
- Junior use of AI for code generation without explicit supervision or authorization
- Proposing tools, libraries, or agents without assessment or governance
- Treating QA as task execution instead of quality ownership
- Treating performance, accessibility or cloud behaviour as someone else’s job or a late-phase activity
- Using observability only reactively, after incidents in production

## Globant community expectation

The Globant QA team is expected not only to deliver in its assigned products, but also to act as
a multiplier of QA best practices across:

- Deliver in assigned squads and products
- Act as multiplier of QA best practices
- Collaborate across Iberia areas and supplier teams
- Reinforce standards through joint in-person sessions with QA Manager
- Contribute to a shared QA community of practice

This expectation is reinforced through the joint in-person sessions led by the QA Manager with
the full QA team, designed to align standards, strengthen quality culture, improve
collaboration, and create a shared community of practice.

## Automation, AI & CoP expectation

- QA roles are expected to work within Iberia’s approved automation stack, currently including Karate, WebdriverIO, and TypeScript
- QA professionals must adapt to new approved tools adopted in the short-to-medium term and help promote their correct use across Iberia
- AI must be used responsibly, critically, and efficiently
- As seniority grows, QA is expected to improve prompts, optimize token usage, refine agent instructions/tools/skills, and contribute to reusable AI-enabled assets
- From Semi-Senior onwards, active participation in Communities of Practice, improvement proposals, and contribution to PoCs is expected
- Any new tool, library, framework, agent, or AI-enabled asset proposal must be assessed and approved under QA governance
