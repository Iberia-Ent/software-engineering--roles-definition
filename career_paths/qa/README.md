# Quality Assurance · Academy Iberia

## Purpose

Explain how QA contribution evolves from guided execution to product-quality leadership and help
stakeholders find the expectations for each level and competency.

## Audience

Product Owner, Product Manager, Delivery Lead, and Technical Lead working with Academy QA
Engineers. QA Manager, Coach, Mentor, and QA Engineer use the same reference for
discipline-specific review.

## Overview

QA is responsible for informed quality judgement, not simply executing test cases or increasing
automation counts. The November 2026 matrix describes eleven competency areas and expects
autonomous proficiency at L3. Performance, accessibility, cloud awareness, and observability
start at L1 and become part of normal quality decisions as seniority grows.

For product stakeholders, the important progression is from reporting issues with support to
independently explaining risk, challenging weak delivery conditions, and influencing quality
practices beyond a single squad. Evidence should show why coverage was chosen, what was learned,
and how unresolved risks were communicated.

## How stakeholders should use this path

Review the level page alongside real refinements, defect reports, test evidence, and
release-risk conversations. The QA Engineer should be able to explain business impact in terms
the team can act on. Technical assessment belongs with QA leadership and the relevant reviewers;
a green automation report alone does not demonstrate mature quality ownership.

The November matrix and the May 2026 chapter are distinct source versions. The matrix provides
the detailed current competency and promotion expectations; the older chapter supplies
complementary responsibility and soft-skill descriptions. They differ in some supervision
wording, and the chapter marks L5 calibration as pending. Do not silently combine those
statements into a new assessment rule; resolve material differences with QA leadership.
