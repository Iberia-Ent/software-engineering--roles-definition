# Frontend Developer · Academy Iberia

## Purpose

Help product stakeholders understand how Frontend contribution evolves from guided
implementation to technical leadership, and find the criteria that support each seniority
discussion.

## Audience

Product Owner, Product Manager, Delivery Lead, and Technical Lead working with Academy Frontend
Developers. Coach, Mentor, and Frontend Developer use this material for technical calibration.

## Overview

Frontend work affects the usability, accessibility, performance, and maintainability of customer
journeys. Increasing seniority should be visible in the ability to deliver those outcomes with
less supervision and to improve how the team delivers, not simply in the number of frameworks
used.

The source defines shared criteria and two coexisting tracks: SPA Legacy (React/Angular) and
Astro + AEM. Confirm the person's actual track before assessing technical evidence. Strength in
one track does not automatically demonstrate proficiency in the other.

## How stakeholders should use this path

Connect observations to product work: how clearly the Developer understands acceptance criteria,
communicates blockers, handles changing requirements, and protects user experience. Ask the
Technical Lead or Coach to validate implementation quality and the technical criteria linked
from the level page.

Examples from reviews, accessibility checks, delivery decisions, and mentoring provide a more
useful discussion than a self-reported skills checklist. Review both the outcome and the support
needed to achieve it. A completed change does not demonstrate autonomous delivery if another
person supplied the essential decisions throughout the work.

The source specifies 80% hard skills and 100% soft skills for L1–L4, with soft skills
demonstrated or in progress and none unobserved. L5's final promotion gate remains pending
calibration.

The source also defines a specific Track A-to-B transition. Do not infer a promotion or level
change solely from a project assignment; the technical criteria for the destination track must
be validated independently.

## Progression in product delivery

At the earlier levels, the team provides explicit boundaries, established implementation
patterns, and regular review. Progress is visible in reliable execution, learning from feedback,
and increasingly effective investigation before escalation.

At intermediate levels, the Developer takes greater ownership of the complete change,
explains technical choices, and maintains quality when requirements or priorities change.

At advanced levels, contribution extends beyond individual implementation. Technical decisions,
mentoring, standards, and collaboration should improve the team's ability to deliver usable,
secure, accessible, and maintainable products.

These descriptions explain the direction of progression. They do not replace the detailed
criteria or create additional promotion requirements.
